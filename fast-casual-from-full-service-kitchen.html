Fast Casual Done Right: Bringing Full-Service Recipes to the Counter

Fast casual has a reputation problem: speed usually comes at the expense of quality. Spring Chicken exists to prove it doesn’t have to. Born from the same kitchen culture as Yardbird, it brings scratch cooking to a counter-service format — and it’s taught us a lot about how to scale quality.

Start with a tight menu

Every additional item slows the line and complicates prep. A focused menu — fried chicken, sandwiches, bowls and sides — lets a team master fewer things and deliver them faster and more consistently.

“Speed shouldn’t come at the expense of quality. Scratch cooking is how we prove it.”

Keep the from-scratch mindset

Brining, breading and sauces made in-house are what separate a great fried chicken sandwich from an average one. It’s a discipline we carried over from our full-service kitchens, and it’s the reason guests taste the difference.

Design packaging and flow for the guest

Fast casual is judged by the moment the food is handed over. Packaging that keeps chicken crisp, portions that travel and a line that moves are all part of the recipe.

Build for growth

Spring Chicken now operates in Miami, Dallas and Las Vegas. Systems for prep, training and quality control are what allow a small-format brand to expand without losing what made it worth expanding.

Private Dining vs. Restaurant Buyout: How to Choose the Right Space

When a client calls about an event, the first question we ask is simple: how many people, and how exclusive does it need to feel? The answer usually points to one of three formats — a private dining room, a semi-private space, or a full restaurant buyout. Each has a sweet spot.

Private dining rooms: 12 to 30 guests

A true private room is enclosed, quiet and built for conversation — ideal for executive dinners, client entertainment and celebrations. At Wakuda in Las Vegas, the private dining room seats 12 to 14. At Chica, two private rooms host 12 to 30 guests with AV capabilities and can be joined by an airwall for larger parties.

“Choose a private room when conversation is the point, and a buyout when the brand experience matters as much as the meal.”

Semi-private spaces: 30 to 100 guests

Semi-private areas keep the energy of a working restaurant while giving your group its own corner of it. Chica’s lounge suits about 35 for networking events and cocktail parties, its library holds 30 to 40, and the bar accommodates up to 100 for receptions. At Wakuda, the secret bar hosts up to 30 guests for sake, Japanese whisky and craft cocktails, and the terrace opens to as many as 150 with views of Las Vegas Boulevard.

Full buyouts: 300+ guests

A buyout hands you the entire restaurant — lighting, music, layout and menu — for product launches, large receptions and milestone celebrations. Our Las Vegas restaurants accommodate up to 325 guests at Chica and up to 400 at Wakuda for reception-style events.

How to decide

Choose a private room when conversation is the point. Choose a semi-private space when you want atmosphere and flexibility. Choose a buyout when brand experience matters as much as the meal. If you’re unsure, tell us your guest count, date and goal — that’s usually enough to recommend the right fit.
